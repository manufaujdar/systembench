03 · Failure modes

Why most benchmarks feel convincing—and fail in use.

The central problem is not that benchmarks are too hard or too easy. It is that they often measure a clean artifact while deployment is a changing relationship between people and a system.

01

Static tasks miss adaptation

One-shot prompts do not test clarification, learning, interruption, correction, or recovery.

02

The model is graded, not the system

Interfaces, tools, policies, retrieval, latency, retries, and operators disappear from the score.

03

Averages hide severe failures

A leaderboard number conceals variance, unsafe actions, tail latency, and worst-user slices.

04

Resources are not matched

Tokens, tools, human help, retries, and time differ while the result is presented as capability.

05

Judges can be circular

Automated evaluators may reward familiar providers, styles, verbosity, or their own preferences.

06

Clean inputs are not human work

Real goals are incomplete, contradictory, multilingual, time-pressured, and embedded in prior state.

07

Public tasks invite gaming

Known examples and rubrics can be memorized, leaked, or directly optimized.

08

Operations are removed

Rate limits, stale data, malformed tools, partial outages, concurrency, and drift vanish.

Freeze before comparison

Version the target, scenarios, budgets, evaluators, exclusions, trials, and seeds before viewing candidate results.

Keep humans in the measurement

Track effort, corrections, disagreement, handoffs, and whether users can recover—not just answer preference.

Fail closed

Missing trials, malformed evidence, unmatched resources, or broken integrity should block a claim instead of creating a score.

Report distributions

Preserve every failure and show uncertainty, Reliability@N, tail behavior, and worst relevant slices.

Harness smoke test

Run the bundled offline system.

This verifies that the runner, traces, fallback checks, aggregation, and strict report path work. It is deliberately not a model benchmark.
